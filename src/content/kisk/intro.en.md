## Who am I?

I studied Information Technology at the Faculty of Information Technology, Brno University of Technology, where I built a technical foundation: application development, working with data, and designing software solutions. For the second year now I have been working as a UX designer at Thermo Fisher Scientific, designing interfaces for technically demanding applications connected to electron microscopes. My work covers interface design, usability testing of existing applications, and finding ways to make complex information accessible to the people who work with it.

## Why KISK?

During projects at FIT, I enjoyed more and more the part that was not only technical: interface design, presenting information clearly, and the relationship between people and technology. It shaped my bachelor’s thesis on the usability of an application for analysing traffic data, where I realised that interface design is not just aesthetics but mainly organising information and supporting users’ decisions. At KISK I want to go deeper in this direction; I am most interested in the Information Service Design specialisation.

## What do I expect?

I am largely self-taught, so I expect the studies to give me the theoretical and methodological base I am still missing, and I am open to everything the field has to offer. In my first three weeks I have started to see design more broadly: not only as applications, but as something that makes people’s lives easier and better, including people with special needs. Designing physical things and services is completely new to me, so I look forward to learning new things and becoming a better designer overall.

## What is my goal?

I want to keep working in UX, usability and the design of digital services, but not only “drawing screens”. I want to understand better how people actually work with information systems: how they look for information, how they make decisions, where they run into problems and what helps them find their way. I am interested in user research, information behaviour, accessibility and working with complex data. I want to connect my technical IT background with a deeper understanding of users. Compared to my bachelor’s thesis, I would like my diploma thesis to focus less on implementation and more on the research and analytical side of the problem.
