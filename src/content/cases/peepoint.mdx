<CaseSection n={1} label="Topic" title="A basic need that becomes stressful in unfamiliar places.">

PeePoint is a product concept for a web and mobile app that helps users find public restrooms quickly and plan toilet stops before and during travel. The idea came from a simple but often ignored need: when people are in an unfamiliar city, park, museum area, or transport hub, they want reassurance that a restroom is nearby if needed.

This need becomes especially important for families with children, seniors, people with health-related requirements, tourists moving between landmarks, and travelers who cannot rely on private facilities. The concept therefore focuses not only on finding the nearest toilet, but also on cleanliness, accessibility, opening hours, ratings, and route planning.

</CaseSection>

<CaseSection n={2} label="User research" title="Survey findings turned a small inconvenience into a product opportunity." titleWidth="24ch">
  <Figure slot="after" src="/images/peepoint/survey-insights.png" alt="Sticky-note survey findings for public restroom finder app" label="Survey synthesis" caption="Key survey questions and findings covering restroom importance, travel mode, planning preferences, QR-code transfer, and feature priorities." />

The research explored how people think about public restroom access when visiting new places. Respondents considered easy access very important and described poor signage, lack of public toilets near landmarks, and difficulty locating standalone toilets not connected to large facilities as common issues.

<p class="emph">The strongest finding was that users wanted different modes for planning and travel. Many preferred planning on a laptop because it gives a larger overview, but wanted to access saved information on a mobile device while already outside.</p>

- Top priorities were 24/7 availability, cleanliness, and accessibility.
- Families requested changing tables, comfort ratings, and reliable opening/access information.

</CaseSection>

<CaseSection n={4} label="Competitive analysis" title="Existing apps solve parts of the problem, but rarely the whole journey." titleWidth="24ch">

We reviewed several existing restroom, map, and travel products, including Where is the Toilet, Toilet Finder, WC Toilet and Restroom Finder, Ivie – Vienna Guide, Google Maps, TripAdvisor, WC Kompas, and Flush Public Toilet/Restrooms.

The analysis showed useful patterns such as distance-based lists, user ratings, GPS navigation, offline functionality, and large toilet databases. At the same time, many tools had distracting ads, unclear filters, limited coverage, no offline mode, missing information about accessibility or changing tables, or poor support for trip planning.

<CaseSection n={5} label="Product specification" title="A connected web and mobile experience." print>

The target application is designed as an intuitive web and mobile app. The web version supports comfortable trip planning on a larger screen, while the mobile version supports quick access during travel. A QR-code transfer lets users move saved restroom locations from desktop planning into the mobile app without repeating the same work.

<Flow steps={[
  { label: 'Before travel · laptop', title: 'Plan & save' },

The core frontend components are an interactive map, search and filtering, reviews and ratings, a personal saved list, and QR-code data transfer. Together, these functions support both immediate needs and planned travel behaviour.

- Interactive map with current location and nearby restroom markers.
- Search and filters for cleanliness, accessibility, opening hours, price/access restrictions, and location type.
- Reviews and ratings to support quick decision-making.
- My List for saving preferred restroom locations across web and mobile.
- QR-code transfer for moving planned locations from laptop to phone.

</CaseSection>

<CaseSection n={6} label="Mobile mockups · 2024" title="Translating the concept into mobile screens.">

The mobile app mockups focus on fast on-the-go use: a map-first interface, switchable list view, toilet detail bottom sheet, filters, adding a toilet, ratings, saved list, and QR-based connection with locations planned on desktop.

The interface keeps key actions close to the map. Users can navigate to a selected restroom, save changes, filter by practical criteria such as price, accessibility, changing table, location type, and review stars, or add missing toilet information to improve the database.

</CaseSection>

The goal was not to let AI decide the product direction, but to use it as a fast prototyping tool: I kept the original research logic, core flows, and feature priorities, then refined the screens into a more consistent map, list, filter, and add-toilet experience.

<CaseSection n={8} label="Next steps" title="From research specification to interface prototype." dashed>

The next step would be to translate the specification into low-fidelity web and mobile flows: planning a route on desktop, saving restroom stops, transferring the list by QR code, and opening the same route on mobile while traveling.

<Quote>The most important validation point would be whether users understand the relationship between planning mode and travel mode, and whether filters such as accessibility, changing tables, cleanliness, and opening hours are visible without overwhelming the map interface.</Quote>

</CaseSection>
